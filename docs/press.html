Long — ~150 words

NoireBox is an open-source black box for AI agents, built solo in the Vosges, France, and published under the MIT licence. It answers one question: if someone contests a decision made by an AI agent tomorrow, who can prove what the agent exactly did — and that the record has not been touched since? Observability tools are built for debugging, and their logs stay editable by whoever holds the disk. NoireBox seals every event into an append-only journal that is hash-chained (SHA-256), signed (Ed25519) and anchored to external RFC 3161 time-stamp authorities, then exports an attestation a third party verifies offline, without trusting the operator. It is the integrity layer that the EU AI Act (art. 12, automatic logging) and the GDPR (art. 5(2), accountability) assume but do not specify. Since 19 September 2026: 21 releases, 428 automated tests, 28 public architecture decision records — each with the alternatives it rejected.

What it does

  • Seals every agent event — append-only, no UPDATE, no DELETE; 0.17 ms per sealed event
  • Localizes falsification — rewrite, insert, delete are detected at the exact event
  • Non-repudiation — Ed25519; the key can live off the agent's disk (key-holder daemon, HSM/PKCS#11)
  • External time — RFC 3161 multi-TSA anchoring + OpenTimestamps (Bitcoin); only 32-byte hashes leave the machine
  • Guards the transcript — 3 engines (regex, trained micro-models, local LLM judge), detection in French and English
  • Names every unprovable state instead of merging it into an OK

What it is not

  • Not a blockchain — one issuer, one verifier, no consensus tax
  • Not observability — debugging is not proving
  • Not a certification — a component that states its own scope (threat model)
  • Not SaaS — self-hosted, zero telemetry, one SQLite file per journal
  • Not a truth oracle — it proves a record was not altered and when it existed; a proof tool, not a talisman

§The story

The question. Your AI agent writes meeting notes that bind your clients, signs purchase orders, files tickets. Eighteen months from now — who can prove what it exactly produced, on which input, with which tool, and why?

The gap. Today's answers — application logs, databases, git histories, agent observability dashboards — share one flaw: they are editable by whoever holds the disk, and the operator can always re-sign. The EU AI Act requires automatic event logs for high-risk systems (art. 12) and the GDPR requires demonstrable accountability (art. 5(2)); neither says how a log proves it was not edited afterwards. No logging-integrity standard is published yet.

The answer. Apply the Certificate-Transparency model to agent journals. NoireBox seals events into an append-only SQLite journal, hash-chained and Ed25519-signed, and periodically anchors the chain head to independent time witnesses (RFC 3161 authorities, OpenTimestamps/Bitcoin). The verifier is a standalone script: an auditor recomputes the whole chain offline, without access to your server, your keys or your trust.

The scope, stated up front. NoireBox proves a record was not altered and when it existed. It does not prove the record was true when written. Every state it cannot prove is named — undecidable is a first-class outcome, never folded into a green check.

§Story angles

Four angles that work, each with the sources a journalist can check in public.

1 — The black box, literally

Interpretability research asks why a model answered as it did — and the field itself warns that a model's self-explanation can be generated after the fact. NoireBox does not open the model. It seals what happened around it — what was asked, what came back, which tool ran, who judged the output and against which specification — and makes that record provable to a third party. The AI Act requires that second kind of transparency now, including where the first remains out of reach. Sources: ART12-MAPPING.md, COMPLIANCE-EU.md, decision receipts (ADR 028 in ADRs.md).

2 — AI agents' logs are editable — and the AI Act demands logs

The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) set the high-risk (Annex III) obligations — including art. 12 logging and ≥ 6-month log retention — for 2 December 2027. It did not defer art. 12 or GDPR art. 5(2). Fines reach €35 M or 7% of global turnover. And no standard yet says what "integrity of logs" means — a solo developer shipped a working answer under MIT, fourteen months before the deadline. Sources: COMPLIANCE-EU.md (verified against consolidated texts, cross-checked 17 Oct 2026), ART12-MAPPING.md, the essay "The AI Act went live two months ago. Your agent's logs are still editable."

3 — Audited in public, by its critics

The project applies "proof, not promises" to itself. Unix veteran Randal L. Schwartz published a catalog of attacks on AI coding-agent gates; NoireBox replayed it as tests in the open — five attack fixtures, sixteen tests, the caught attacks visible in the test names and the gaps named in code rather than smoothed over. Schwartz then named the underlying principle "Sam LABBE's Corollary": two engineers, two stacks (Unix primitives vs cryptographic ledgers), the same four defenses. An independent reviewer on dev.to re-ran the verifier release after release; each finding became a shipped answer within days, and the full audit of v0.20.0 — ten findings — was answered publicly, point by point. Sources: test_scar_replay.py, SCAR-BENCH.md, AUTHORITY-LADDER.md, "Ten findings, ten answers".

4 — A proof tool, not a talisman

The rare story of a product that states what it cannot do. NoireBox proves integrity and existence-in-time; it refuses to vouch for truth. Twenty-one releases in one month, every architectural decision public — twenty-eight ADRs, each with what was rejected and why — and the whole method MIT-licensed, because the maker's position is: the journal stays local, the repo ships the method, verification stays free forever. Sources: ADRs.md, THREAT-MODEL.md, CHANGELOG.md.
