04 · STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Before you ask —
the awkward ones.

Does the core stay free?

Yes — MIT, forever, verification included. That's not generosity, it's the trust guarantee: a proof tool whose math sits behind a paywall proves nothing. Paid things live in the witness layer, never in the cryptography.

What exactly leaves my infrastructure?

32-byte chain heads — the fingerprints, never the events. The hub cannot reconstruct a single payload from a hash; there is nothing to leak, subpoena or mishandle. That's why the witness layer can be a service while the journal stays sovereign.

Do I need qualified timestamps?

Not for most needs: public TSAs and Bitcoin anchoring are free and cryptographically strong. Qualified (eIDAS art. 41) adds a legal presumption — worth it for regulated and high-risk deployments. The configuration ships in the core today; the managed qualified tier is on the waitlist.

Is the Sprint a certification?

No. NoireBox is not a notified body, not a QTST, and issues no certificate of conformity. The Sprint leaves you with the evidence infrastructure and the auditor dossier for your own assessment — the same perimeter its docs state everywhere else.

Why pay for anchoring at all?

Because "when" is the weak link: a self-issued date is the suspect signing its own report, and an unanchored chain only proves integrity — never existence-at-a-date. Running that witness properly (cadence, rotation, multi-TSA redundancy, retention, drift alerting) is an operations discipline. You're paying for the discipline, not for the hash.
